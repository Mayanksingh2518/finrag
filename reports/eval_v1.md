# FinRAG evaluation v1 (Phase 7 summary)

Generated by `python -m app.evaluation.build_eval_report` from the committed reports; no model calls. Golden set: 84 questions over 40 10-K filings (10 companies, FY2022-2025): 27 lookup, 17 numeric, 12 trend, 12 comparison, 6 follow-up, 10 unanswerable; quote-verified gold pages; hand-written reference answers.

## Headline

- **Answers:** judged correctness (all answerable, abstention = 0) 0.730 → **0.865** from agent v0 to the routed v2 agent (v0 judged by gpt-oss-120b; v2 scores 0.865 by gpt-oss and by an independent qwen3.8-27b judge that wrote no answer); trend 0.250 → 0.792, comparison 0.667 → 0.958 / 1.000.
- **Retrieval:** hybrid (dense + BM25, RRF) + cross-encoder rerank + a per-entity retrieval plan: recall@10 0.966, MRR 0.927 with gold filters.
- **Grounding:** every claim is checked against its cited page in code; abstention recall 1.000 (all 10 unanswerable questions refused), false abstention 0.041.
- **Cost:** zero. Local embeddings, reranker and 3B model; Groq free tier for trend/comparison answers and for the judges; every LLM call cached.

Reading: BM25 alone misses paraphrases, dense alone misses exact terms; RRF fusion beats both, and the cross-encoder adds the most (MRR 0.825 → 0.933) at ~2 s per query. Without filters plain retrieval collapses (BM25 recall@5 0.471); with the reranker it barely moves (0.884), which is what makes the agent's own filters safe. The plan's per-entity quotas fix multi-company/multi-year questions where one entity crowded out the others.

Reading:
- The agent finds its own filters and rewrites follow-ups without losing anything against gold filters (false abstention 0.054 = 0.054); without XBRL facts it would (0.095).

- Trend was the weak spot (0.250). Code-computed series tables and intent rules took it to 0.542; the rest was model capacity, so trend/comparison questions are routed to gpt-oss-120b (0.792 / 1.000). A stricter "never compute a percentage" prompt was tested and rejected: more abstentions, lower correctness.

Free-tier capacity is set by the routed path: ~4k tokens per routed answer against Groq's ~200k tokens/day per model, about 50 routed answers a day before the local model takes over.

## 5. Known limits

- **Definition mismatches:** XBRL line items vs company definitions (Meta capex incl. finance leases, Amazon net cash capex, Apple buyback program vs cash paid): T12 (Meta capex trend) and N16 (Amazon capex) are still judged incorrect; T11, N09 and F06 are now correct.
- **Small golden set:** 84 questions, 12 per hard category, so one question moves a category score by ~0.08.
- **Labels and references by the same assistant** that wrote the judge prompt; a human review of the 22 labels would make the judge validation independent.
- **Judge cost:** ~1.3-1.6k tokens per answer; a full judged run uses half a day of one Groq model's quota.
- **RAGAS on 26 of 74 answers** (free-tier time); the full run is resumable from the cache.
- **Tracing to Langfuse** is implemented but only tested against a mock until keys are added.
